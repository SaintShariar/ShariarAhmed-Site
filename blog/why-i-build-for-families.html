Why I build for underprivileged families

Every project I start has the same north star: clearer tools, fairer access, and real opportunity for people who get left behind by default.

I grew up watching how hard families work when systems aren’t built for them. Opportunity isn’t evenly distributed — and neither is information, time, or trust. If a product only works for people who already have those things, it isn’t helping the people who need it most.

That’s why I self-start. Waiting for permission rarely serves the communities I care about. Founder energy, for me, means spotting a gap — in budgeting, connection, access — and building something practical enough that a household can use it without a manual.

Impact over aesthetics

Design matters. Polish matters. But the first question is always: who does this serve, and can they actually use it? A budget tracker that helps a family see clearly beats a flashy demo that looks good in a portfolio and dies in the real world.

Community work through organizations like UAMA, Lions International, and civic campaigns reinforced the same lesson: show up, listen, and leave people better equipped than you found them.

What I’m building toward

I’m not interested in technology for its own sake. I’m interested in tools that gather people, open doors, and make the next step less confusing. That’s the work — and it’s the reason this site, these projects, and this blog exist.
